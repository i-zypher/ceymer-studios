The idea

Time, but tuned differently.

The hydrogen line — 1420.405751 MHz — is the emission frequency of neutral hydrogen, the most abundant element in the universe. Radio astronomers point dishes at it to map galaxies. SETI proposed it as the natural channel for interstellar communication. It is, in a real sense, the most universal frequency we know.

Hydrogen Beat takes that frequency and uses it as a basis for a different unit of time: the Beat. One day is divided into exactly 100 Beats. No hours, no minutes, no seconds — just a single, fluid number from 00.00 to 99.99 that moves smoothly through your day.

The math

How a Beat works.

1 day = 86,400 seconds
1 day = 100 Beats
→ 1 Beat = 864 seconds (14 min 24 sec)
Each Beat subdivides decimally → 0.01 Beat = 8.64 seconds

It's clean, it's symmetric, and once you start glancing at it you stop thinking in arbitrary sexagesimal-meets-decimal hybrids. You start feeling the day as one continuous gradient.

The build · Real talk

What it took to ship something this strange.

War story · 01

The Wear OS chapter that didn't work out

Hydrogen Beat seemed like a natural fit for a watch face — what's more "watch" than time itself? A serious Wear OS build attempt followed. Several weeks of work.

It was ultimately abandoned. The Samsung Wear OS platform limitations — around watch face APIs, custom rendering, and what an app is actually allowed to do — made the experience I wanted impossible without compromises that gutted the concept. The phone app stayed; the watch face shelved. Sometimes the best engineering decision is to walk away from a platform that won't let you build the thing properly.

War story · 02

Making "strange" feel obvious

The hardest UX problem with a novel timekeeping system isn't displaying the time — it's making someone who's used 24-hour days their entire life able to glance at 47.32 and intuit what that means. The current build leans on visual context (the wave position, percentage-style framing) so the number lands without needing a conversion chart.

War story · 03

A passion project that taught me discipline

Hydrogen Beat was never going to be a unicorn app. That was clear from day one. But it taught me more about shipping a complete product through Google Play — signing, ProGuard, store listing, screenshots, content rating, privacy policy — than any tutorial could. The experimental nature was a feature.
