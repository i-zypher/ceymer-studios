The world

A city that hums.

Hum City is a place. It is also a sound — the constant low resonance of a city wired so completely that you can feel it in your teeth. Power lines, signal towers, the underground infrastructure of a metropolis that never decided where its hardware ended and its people began.

It's a system for people, not profit.

The stories of Hum City are about the people who live inside that signal — what they want, what they're willing to do to get it, who they betray along the way, and what's left of them when the broadcast finally cuts out.

The cast

Three figures in the signal.

The easiest way to meet someone in Hum City is to find out who's been writing about them — what the council prints, what the corporations file, what the threat boards circulate. Three records. Three figures. Three institutions doing the talking.

Calyx Tyrell
Co-Founder, Nexweave Systems Senior Council Member · 152 years

"A system that cannot change is not a system. It is a monument. And monuments don't keep anyone warm."

Hum City Civic Record
Office of the Council · Honoree Volume IX
Public Record · Council-Authorized
Background

Born into a lineage of data theorists and rogue engineers who believed that code was not written but grown, Councilor Tyrell has spent over a century at the intersection of consciousness, technology, and civic architecture. Raised in an environment where biological data and digital systems were treated as inseparable, he developed a foundational philosophy that would eventually reshape how an entire city thinks about itself.

Foundational Work

As co-founder of Nexweave Systems, Tyrell pioneered the development of Soulcode — adaptive, semi-sentient architecture that responds to human behavior rather than simply processing it. Where other firms built tools, Nexweave built presence. The company grew under his guidance from a fringe data archive into one of the most significant infrastructure systems underpinning Hum City's daily function.

Service to the City

As one of Hum City's original council members, Councilor Tyrell has served through every era of the city's evolution — the idealism of its founding, the growing pains of its expansion, and the difficult systemic reforms that have shaped the modern city. He remains one of the few figures who has held continuous influence across the full arc of Hum City's history, and is widely regarded as a stabilizing force during its most turbulent transitions.

Present Service

At 152 years old, Councilor Tyrell continues to hold an active seat on the council, where he is known for his measured temperament, long-view thinking, and unflinching commitment to what he calls functional governance — the belief that a city that cannot adapt to survive is not worth preserving. He is considered one of the most beloved public figures in Hum City's history.

— Hum City Civic Record · Office of the Council · Honoree Volume IX

Vendra-Konn™ Internal Affairs Division

Physical Description

Subject presents as female, approximately nineteen years of age, standing at roughly five feet in height — a detail that has apparently done nothing to slow her down. Hair: neon green, typically worn up, and by multiple field reports dense enough to conceal at minimum one full-size tool. Eyes: described variously as "large," "unsettling," and "weirdly sincere" — usually obscured behind a pair of glowing blue optical goggles that emit an audible whirring sound when active.

Her clothing has been described in surveillance footage as — and this is a direct quote from three separate VendBot™ field reports — "like a scrapyard exploded at a rave." She carries a long, flexible cable she has been observed using as a lasso, a climbing aid, a restraint device, and on at least one occasion, a jump rope.

The Core Offense

The subject is conducting a sustained, unauthorized intrusion campaign against Vendra-Konn™ vending infrastructure across multiple city districts. Units she interfaces with cease to function as revenue-generating assets. Product is dispensed without transaction. Data collection is interrupted. In several cases, units have required full wipes and firmware reinstallation before returning to operational status.

She is not stealing in any conventional sense. She is not extracting product for resale. The losses are structural. Every unit she touches stops doing what it was built to do, and starts doing something else entirely — something this office is not prepared to detail in a general circulation document.

Field Engagement

On three separate occasions, VendBot™ enforcement units dispatched to intercept the subject were physically restrained using her cable and left inoperable at the scene. In one incident, a VendBot™ unit was reprogrammed mid-confrontation. Recovery teams found it in a state this office has declined to include in the official maintenance log.

Current Status

She does not have a corpchip implant. She does not appear in the BioData census. She does not exist in any corporate registry. She does, however, exist on our surveillance footage. Extensively.

This matter has been personally escalated to Priority One by Gristo Vendra, Chief Executive. We will find her. We will have answers.

— Vendra-Konn™ Internal Affairs · "Protecting the Snack. Securing the Future."

Nexweave Systems · Seam Security Division
Public Threat Advisory · Council Mandate 7.1

Subject Background

Davies is a product of the Vever community — a closed, unregistered cultural enclave operating in Hum City's lower infrastructure districts. The Vevers do not cooperate with civic oversight, do not participate in BioData census registration, and actively resist transparency with regulatory authorities. What is known about their internal structure, hierarchy, and practices has been assembled from incident reports alone. They have provided nothing voluntarily.

Davies himself carries no registered BioData identity. He holds no licensed jack permit. He has no corporate enrollment, no civic record, and no verifiable history prior to his first documented offense. By every measure this city uses to account for its citizens, he does not exist.

Documented Offenses

Davies has carried out multiple confirmed infiltrations against protected Nexweave infrastructure. These are not acts of civil disobedience. They are not protests. They are deliberate, targeted attacks against systems that underpin the data security of every licensed individual in this city.

During his most significant incursion, Davies breached the outer Seam Archive vaults — restricted soulcode storage holding the protected data of Nexweave clients and personnel. He incapacitated multiple licensed Loom operatives on active duty. He left each with vèvè glyph markers burned directly into their interface hardware — a form of sabotage requiring complete vault wipes and causing significant operational disruption.

Assessment

His intrusion method does not conform to any licensed interface protocol on record. His jack rig is unlicensed, self-constructed from salvaged components, and modified in ways that continue to resist full technical analysis. The glyph structures he embeds into compromised systems have caused cascading failures in biometric vault logic, disrupted soulcode containment architecture, and in documented cases rendered Nexweave personnel non-responsive for extended periods.

He is not a mercenary. He is not corporate-backed. He is answerable to no authority that this city can currently reach. That is precisely why this advisory exists.

— Nexweave Systems · Office of the Director · Authorized under Council Mandate 7.1

Ongoing
Why bother

Worldbuilding is the long game.

Software gets shipped, used, and superseded. Worlds don't. A story that lands stays in someone's head for years. A character people care about outlives the platform they were introduced on. Hum City is the project John and I expect to be working on for decades — the slow, patient counterweight to a career spent shipping apps.

Collaboration changes the shape of it. A solo writer can be exact; two writers building the same world have to be honest with each other — about what works, what doesn't, what the city sounds like when neither of us is the one talking. The result is bigger than either of us would make alone, and more strange.

Hum City is still being broadcast.
