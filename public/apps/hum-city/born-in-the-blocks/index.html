Born in the Blocks

Chapter Twenty-One · Hum City, 2201 / The Blocks

I. How She Told It

I don't remember being born. Obviously. Because that would be weird and also probably traumatic. But Mom told me the story so many times it's basically a memory now.

I'm gonna write it exactly how she told it—with all her tangents, and the parts where her voice got soft-loud. You know the thing. Where it's quiet but somehow BIGGER, the way it got when she said my name.

Because if Future Me ever forgets what Mom sounds like, Past Me will literally haunt her.

Someone should know Mom's voice. Someone should know she existed, and loved me, and that that matters more than anything else I'm about to tell you.

So. Here's how she told it.

II. The DocBot

The midwife was a traveling DocBot. One of those repurposed military med-units that rolls through the Blocks doing births and emergency patches for people who can't afford a real hospital.

Which was everybody.

People just called him DocBot, but his actual designation was DocBot-7734. Mom said he still had this nervous hum in his servos whenever something went wrong—like his programming couldn't quite forget that, once, a bad outcome used to mean something very different. He'd been a military triage unit before the war ended, before somebody scrubbed his combat protocols and wiped most of what he used to be and taught him how to deliver babies instead of the other thing. The delivering-death thing.

He hummed A LOT during my birth, apparently.

III. The Blocks Were Burning

The power went out twice while Mom was in labor. The vending machine three floors down kept glitching—you could hear it from our apartment—dispensing free SynthMallow Blobs and playing its broken jingle on a loop.

Mom said that was the universe apologizing for the timing. Her water broke during a brownout, and the Blocks were burning again.

Not the fun kind of burning. The bad kind.

Some gang beef. Some corporate coup. Some reason that sounded real important to people with guns and meant absolutely fuck-all to people just trying not to die in a one-room apartment that smelled like mildew and smoke.

It was just her and DocBot. Dad wasn't in the picture. Mom never talked about him much, except to say, "Some people are good at leaving, and your father got a degree in it."

I didn't ask questions, because Mom's face did this thing—this little flicker, like a bad connection—whenever the topic came up, and I did not want to be the reason for the flicker.

So that was it. Just her, and a nervous bot, and the sound of sirens outside, and the smell of burning plasteel and synthesized strawberry drifting over from the flavor district three blocks down.

That's the world I was born into.

Hi. Hello. It's me.
